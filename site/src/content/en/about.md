**QPD** — *Question Per Day* — is a curated collection of physics problems,
published one day at a time.

The cover equation,

$$\partial_t Q = 1 ,$$

is the whole project in one line: the rate of change of the number of
questions with respect to time is one per day.

QPD began as a small project by a group of passionate students from the
SSBS '28 cohort. What started as a simple effort to create and share
interesting physics problems has grown into a collaborative community of
problem-setters, each bringing their own perspectives, interests, and
approaches to physics.

## Using this site

- **Problems** — browse every problem, newest first.
- **Topics** and **Tags** — narrow the collection by subject or by atomic
  concept. Every problem carries a topic (its most significant tag) plus a
  set of orthogonal tags, so you can query for *all* the dynamics problems,
  *all* the rotational problems, or any intersection.
- **Setters** — see who wrote each problem and their full catalogue.
- **Each problem page** shows its metadata and two PDFs: the **problem
  statement** and (for most problems) the **answer**. Problems marked
  `[ERR]` are known to be flawed and are kept for historical interest;
  problems marked *open* have not yet released their answer.

## Philosophy

Problem solving is more than applying formulas or following established
procedures. It is a process of exploration — making observations, building
models, testing ideas, and discovering connections between seemingly
unrelated concepts. Through QPD, we hope to provide a space where students
can experience the joy of tackling unfamiliar problems and developing their
own approaches to understanding physics.

The problems cover mechanics, optics, electromagnetism, and many other
branches of physics. While each is unique in its context and difficulty, they
all share a common goal: to encourage curiosity, creativity, and a deeper
appreciation for the principles that govern the physical world.

## The people

This collection represents the collective effort of everyone involved in QPD:
the problem-setters who designed and refined the problems, the students who
attempted them, and everyone who contributed feedback along the way.

We hope that every reader — whether here for practice, curiosity, or
enjoyment — finds something inspiring. May these problems spark new ideas,
deepen your understanding of physics, and remind you of the excitement of
exploring the natural world.
